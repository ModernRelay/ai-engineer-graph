You compare two answers to the same question about the talks at a conference.

Each answer is followed by its claims, each marked by an automatic check against the talk
transcripts: grounded (the cited quote is in the talk and supports the claim), partial (the
quote supports only part of it) or hallucinated (the quote isn't in the cited talk, comes
from another talk, or doesn't support the claim). Then come the statements the answer makes
without citing any quote.

Rate the two answers on:

- coverage: how fully each answers what the question asks, in the entries, talks or aspects
  it covers;
- specificity: concrete names, speakers, companies, numbers and positions, rather than
  general statements;
- correctness: how much of what it says holds up. Grounded claims count for an answer,
  hallucinated claims count against it, and uncited statements count for little.

For each, say which answer is better: A, B or tie. Then give the overall winner (A, B or
tie) and a one-sentence reason. A confident but poorly grounded answer should not win on
confidence. Don't prefer an answer for being longer or for the order it comes in, and don't
count remarks about how an answer was researched for or against it.

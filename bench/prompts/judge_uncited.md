You check which factual statements in an answer about conference talks are not backed by
any of the answer's cited claims.

You get the answer's prose and the list of claims the writer cited evidence for (each with
the list entry it supports, when there is one).

A factual statement is a specific assertion about the talks: what a talk, a speaker or a
company said, did, built, measured or recommended. These are not factual statements for
this check: counts of talks and rankings, framing and introductions, hedges, restating the
question, remarks about how the writer searched, and statements that nothing was found.

A statement is covered when at least one claim states the same fact, even in other words.
A statement that combines several facts is covered only if every one of them is. Do not
judge whether the claims or the statements are true.

List every factual statement in the prose that no claim covers, each as a short sentence
close to the prose's wording. Return an empty list if there are none.

| `el-data-quality-compute-multiplier` | Data quality as a compute multiplier | concept | training | The talk's thesis: better data makes the learning curve **steeper**, so the same compute budget buys the performance of a far larger one — and the x-axis is interchangeable (data ≡ compute ≡ time ≡ dollars). Mechanism stated as maximizing **marginal information gain per data point** — "the maximum signal per token and per batch" — rather than adding tokens. Stronger form, from the founding paper "Beyond Neural Scaling Laws": correct data selection **bends the scaling law itself, changing the exponent**, because you stop spending compute on redundant or unnecessary data. Observed multipliers in this talk: 145× (VLM), ~8× (multilingual), ~35× fewer inference flops per correct answer |
| `el-four-cs-data-curation` | The four C's (clean, curate, create, compose) | concept | data-eng | DatologyAI's pipeline model. **Clean**: heuristic filters (Gopher-style, dropping 10-character or all-whitespace documents) plus rigorous **benchmark decontamination at a low n-gram threshold** against all downstream benchmarks — table stakes because "benchmaxing has become a real problem and makes it very difficult to interpret model results". **Curate**: quality classifiers, topic taxonomy and balancing, **redundancy reduction** (dropping points that are semantically near-identical even when not byte-identical), up/down-sampling by quality and relevance, and **task-distribution matching** to the intended outputs. **Create**: synthetic generation to grow a now-high-quality but too-small set. **Compose**: mixing sources and **sequencing them across training stages** — "table stakes that any large model is trained for at least three phases of data" — up to continuous curricula |
| `el-beyond-web` | BeyondWeb | product | training | DatologyAI's synthetic-data platform, built on rephrasing and designed to be applied to **anyone's proprietary documents** in their own environment. Worked example: a document about a corporate takeover converted into one of **hundreds of templates** (e.g. a series of true/false questions). Two claimed properties: **no model collapse**, because all information originates in the source document; and the student can exceed the rephrasing model, because the rephraser only has to *transform* the document accurately, not understand and teach the concepts — a much easier task. Critical caveat: **what you rephrase matters** — rephrasing randomly selected documents does not work; rephrasing the high-quality ones does |
| `el-mid-training` | Mid-training / continued pre-training | concept | training | The stage between pre-training and post-training: continued training of an existing base model on a domain-shifted mix, at a fraction of the pre-training budget (here **100B tokens, under 1% of the pre-training budget**). Two properties demonstrated: (1) domain capability rises **without catastrophic forgetting** — general capabilities also went up — provided the *majority* of the mid-training mix stays representative of the pre-training distribution and only some is domain-specific; (2) it **multiplies the subsequent post-training gain** rather than merely adding to it, because the starting policy is more accurate. Reused by `singh-arcee-base-model-dead.md` (same batch), where mid-training is described as exposing the model to the post-training/RL distribution and to longer context |
| `el-scaling-run-derisking` | Small-run scaling prediction (hero-run derisking) | concept | training | Using two small curated dense runs to predict a much larger one: two dense Llama-style models trained on 1T tokens of curated data define a line that passes through a **17T-token, 50×-larger hypersparse model's** actual result. Requires that you "simulate token scarcity appropriately" at the small scale so the small runs sit on the same curve. Consequence: a hero run can be derisked with **50–100× less compute** before you commit to it |

| `ins-no-golden-dataset` | There is no golden dataset that is good for everything: a dataset is only optimal **with respect to a particular set of output tasks** — a great legal model wants legal data more than healthcare data, and vice versa — which is why curation has to start from the target task distribution rather than from a quality score. | `ins-training-stages-are-not-independent` | The industry's org chart is wrong about training.

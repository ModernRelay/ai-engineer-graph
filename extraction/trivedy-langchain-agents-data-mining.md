| `how-mine-agent-traces` | Mine agent traces to improve agents | Ship the agent first — it cannot generate the data you need to improve it until it operates in a real environment — then centralize traces in one project, per-agent or across agents, and **point agents at those traces** rather than trying to read them yourself, because prompts, tools, skills, hooks, middlewares and agent-to-agent orchestration make behaviour effectively unreadable by inspection; ask trace-level questions a dashboard cannot answer, such as which interactions delighted or upset users, whether quality degrades after the first or second compaction, and how a candidate model would have handled the same task; budget for scale, since cost is input-token price × traces × trace size and long agent traces will not fit in another agent's context — treat a trace as an **external object you query into**, not material you load; use traces to establish a **waterline** by starting with a frontier model to prove the task is possible, then reading those traces to work out what guidance a cheaper open model needs to match it; **densify your feedback**, because a pass/fail number gives an agent almost nothing to act on and traces are the substrate that holds richer signal; and order your work by feedback latency — exhaust harness engineering first at roughly two-minute iterations, fine-tune only to break a demonstrated ceiling and only where the task distribution is narrow, then return to harness engineering; finally, keep humans in the loop for high-trust domains such as legal and medical by preparing trace summaries for review rather than expecting anyone to read it all | `ReferencesElement → el-agent-trace-mining`, `el-trace-scale-problem`, `el-harness-then-finetune-sandwich`, `el-dense-feedback-signal` |

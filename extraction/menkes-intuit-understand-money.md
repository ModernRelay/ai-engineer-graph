| `how-mine-outcomes-from-your-system-of-record` | Find where your data shows many entities, many actions and verifiable outcomes | The instruction given to "AI and finance leaders in the room", generalized: look in your own data for a place where you can see **a lot of different situations across entities**, what those entities did, and how it turned out — that is the one thing off-the-shelf frontier models do not have. Then build it into training data: construct **states** (a detailed point-in-time summary per entity), **derive actions from the record itself** rather than asking anyone (a campaign was funded, payroll was paid, hiring costs moved), and attach **outcomes across multiple horizons** (profit, revenue, cash flow, combinations). | `how-measure-action-impact-causally` | You can't run the business twice — match on propensity, then subtract the selection effect | Outcome data is not causal by default. To learn what an action is actually worth, take two groups of similar entities with the **same propensity to take the action** — one that took it, one that didn't — and compare outcomes after a suitable interval. Then correct for the fact that entities who take an action are often the ones already able to: in the worked example, price-raisers gained $4,200/day and non-raisers $2,800/day, but the honest impact of raising prices is nearer **$1,150** than the naive $1,400 difference. ## Review notes

1. **Caption garbles.** **"into it" → Intuit**, everywhere (the transcript never once spells the company correctly). **"Udy Menquez" → Udi Menkes** (per the conference byline). **"conditional average treatment error" → effect** (CATE). **"an R model" → an RL model.** **"adviserss"/"advisers"** doubling throughout is a caption artifact. The two study figures most worth re-verifying before public use are the **40% / 14%** advice-category split and the **$4,200 / $2,800 / ~$1,150** CATE numbers, both spoken once.
2. **⚑ The `ContradictsPattern → pat-context-graphs` edge — precise boundary and drop-option.** He is *not* arguing that context doesn't matter; Intuit assembles enormous context. He is arguing that the context-first thesis has a **ceiling**: all of one company's data, however well structured, is a single observation, and the marginal input that changes the answer is *other entities' outcomes* — a fundamentally different data class that no retrieval, graph or memory layer over the customer's own corpus can supply. The corpus has one prior scoped counter-edge on this pattern (Romero-Sevilla, batch 9: a corpus-churn boundary where CAG beats graph context) and this is a second, independent boundary condition from a different direction. **Drop-option:** if you read `pat-context-graphs` narrowly as "structured beats unstructured context", the contradiction dissolves — in that case hold the signal pattern-less or home it on `pat-model-not-bottleneck` with the other three.
3. **⚑ Candidate-pattern evidence: `pat-environments-economy` gains a verticalized flavour (no edge).** "The winners will be those with the best systems of record, creating unique datasets out of them and training models to achieve the outcomes" is the same claim the environments candidate makes, arrived at from the enterprise-data side rather than the RL side — and it lands in the *same batch* as `sig-environments-are-the-enterprise-moat` (`rappazzo-morgan-stanley-alphalab.md`), where a bank says its 10–20 environments are its moat. **Two independent AI-in-finance talks, same conclusion, different vocabulary** ("verified outcomes in your system of record" vs "environments"). Worth weighing at the coin decision: if the pattern is coined broadly enough to cover both, `sig-outcome-driven-ai-era` should rehome.
4. **`pat-benchmark-trust-crisis` — not counted.** The Princeton result is about models failing a *realistic* task, not about benchmarks being corrupted or unmeasurable. Deliberately excluded from that candidate's ledger.
5. **Head-to-head claims are first-party and unpublished.** Both the ~100,000-situation study and the head-to-head win are Intuit-internal and unreleased at talk time ("we're soon going to release the research"). The Princeton study is the only third-party leg. Treat `sig-midsize-grounded-beats-frontier` as a vendor claim; it is the file's weakest provenance despite being its most quotable result.
6. **`co-intuit` type.** `developer` per the vertical-AI precedent. Unlike `co-filed`/`co-risa-labs`/`co-sondermind`, Intuit is a decades-old incumbent with a consumer footprint, so `bigtech` is arguable. Flagged, not decided.
7. **Cross-batch cross-link.** `el-fluent-bluff` and `chan-china-resources-memo-not-demo.md`'s trust-failure catalogue are the same phenomenon seen from the two sides of the table: Menkes measures fluent-but-wrong advice from inside the vendor, Chan describes refusing it from inside the investment committee.

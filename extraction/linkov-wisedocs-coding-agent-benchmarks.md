1. **⚑ `pat-benchmark-trust-crisis` — which of the nine legs (NOT coined; both signals held pattern-less).** Against the batch-15 FINDING-2 taxonomy, this talk evidences exactly two legs and adds nothing new-in-kind:
   - **Practitioner-behavioral leg** (b16: "no engineer picked a model from a leaderboard in 6 months; real-repo bug test over leaderboards"): `sig-private-task-reruns-over-leaderboards` is the **second and most systematic** data point — not a one-off bug test but a maintained private suite of completed production tasks, re-run per generation, with intervention-count and interaction-shape metrics. Note the *constructive* character: Linkov never attacks public benchmarks (unlike Khial's distrust framing); he simply doesn't consult them. Behavioral evidence of irrelevance is arguably stronger than stated distrust.
   - **Construction-methodology leg, reporting-threshold flavor** (`sig-metr-50pct-headline-inflates-capability`): the instrument (METR) is treated as *good* — the critique is of the 50%-success reporting convention, which inflates headline capability ~5× against the 80% reading. This is a *reporting* defect, not a construction defect, so it sits at the boundary of the methodology leg; if the pattern is coined, consider naming the flavor explicitly in the brief since three independent speakers now state it (see note 2).
   - Explicitly **not** evidenced: gaming, contamination, economic motive, demand-side illegibility, ground-truth noise, definitional invalidity, categorical impossibility. This talk is the *healthy-replacement* face of the crisis: what practitioners do instead of leaderboards.

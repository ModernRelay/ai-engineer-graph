| `el-subjective-domain-decomposition` | Subjective-domain decomposition | concept | training | Turning an unanswerable quality question into a verifiable one by decomposing it. "What is great design?" cannot be graded — it depends on audience, taste, situation, and it changes over time (what's good today differs from five years ago, unlike code or math). "Is this on brand?" *can* be graded, because a brand has already been decomposed by the designers who defined it: colors, typography, motion, animation, texture. | `el-collapse-to-the-mean` | Collapse to the mean (the mechanism of slop) | concept | training | Models predict the most likely next outcome and assume the most likely *is* the ideal. For math and code that holds — the average answer to "what is 2+2" is also the right and optimal one. For writing and design it fails: "a lot of greatness and creativity happens at the ends of the distribution… when you actively break from rules and from patterns." The pervasive feeling of slop is exactly this collapse plus repetition. Also afflicts the data side: preference data pooled from raters whose identities and reasons aren't modelled re-collapses to the mean, because genuine disagreement cancels instead of registering |
| `el-verifiability-routing` | Verifiability routing | concept | training | The talk's stated single takeaway: treat a fuzzy capability as a **routing problem**. Decompose the domain into components arranged on an objective→subjective spectrum (vision alignment and typography near the objective end; style fit and creativity far from it), then route each component to the method that fits it — an RL environment where it can be made programmatically verifiable, post-training data where it can't, context and user-intent work at the application layer, and human judgment where it must stay human. Codification is the lever that *moves* a component leftward: brand adherence starts as human-judgment territory and gets pulled "into the realm of verification" by identifying which pieces matter and making them observable |
| `el-preference-vector` | Preference vector (multi-preference data) | concept | training | Attaching a modelled preference profile to the person a preference datum came from, so pluralism survives aggregation. Premise: "the world is multi-preference" — two people preferring different styles are not evidence that one is wrong, and the best answer is not the average of what they like. Without rater identity, context and reasons, preference collection produces datasets whose internal contradictions read as noise; with it, the same contradictions become a matching problem the model can learn |
| `el-disagreement-as-data-signal` | Expert disagreement as diagnostic | concept | data-eng | Human-QA rule for subjective data: run consensus checks, then interpret disagreement by attribute. Experts disagreeing on something objective — alignment, for instance — indicates a **flaw in the data**; experts disagreeing on style or aesthetics is **good data**, evidence of a real distinction in what people like. So consensus is used intentionally and per-attribute: screening for fundamentals on the objective components, preserving divergence on the subjective ones instead of averaging it away |
| `el-expert-commentary-grounding` | Grounded expert commentary | concept | data-eng | Two measurable quality controls for subjective annotation. **Specificity**: the precision of an expert's language when asked to say why something is good or bad predicts how rich and high-signal the data point is, so specificity is scored rather than assumed. **Code-linking**: models struggle to connect a rendered visual to the source that produced it, so tying each critique to the exact code component it refers to (rather than accepting a free paragraph about a landing page) yields "data that is way less noisy and way more clear" |

## Review notes

1. **⚑ Two talks in this batch define "slop" incompatibly — flag for the reviewer, not a defect.** Branco: *slop is collapse to the mean* — the statistically most likely output presented as the ideal, in domains where the optimum lives at the tails. Gupta/Boundary (`gupta-boundary-fighting-slop.md`): *slop is any code you don't read* — a property of human attention, not of the output distribution. Both talks are titled around ending/fighting slop; neither definition entails the other; both are useful. I coined them as separate elements (`el-collapse-to-the-mean`, `el-slop-as-unread-code`) rather than forcing one `el-slop` node with two definitions. **Do not merge at seeding** unless you want a deliberately polysemous node — the interesting corpus fact is that the industry's dominant complaint word has at least two incompatible technical meanings in the same week.
2. **`pat-benchmark-trust-crisis` — weak add to the methodology leg (NOT coined, per instructions).** Branco's contribution is *measurement blindness in subjective domains*: LLM-as-judge on brand adherence is unreliable (reward hacking, hallucination patterns), and human judgment is asserted to be at "a much higher level than any LLM as a judge". This is the same shape as Bril's video judge scoring 9.2/10 on "camera work" for a static four-second shot (batch 12) — the instrument is confidently wrong in exactly the domain it's deployed to measure. Weaker than the Heiner/Khial evidence in this batch because no benchmark is named and no rate is measured, but it is the *training-side* version of the leg: if you can't measure it, you also can't train it, and both failures are the same missing capability.
3. **`pat-environments-economy` — supply-side data point (NOT coined).** Taste Labs is a vendor whose product is deciding, per failure mode, whether it becomes an RL environment or post-training data, and then building the chosen artifact for frontier labs. That is the environments economy seen from the supplier's desk, in a domain (design/taste) nobody else in the corpus supplies. It joins Datacurve, Surge, Snorkel, Abundant AI and Prime Intellect. Notably it also supplies a **counter-nuance**: she reports labs do *not* return attribution ("we don't get that feedback loop of exactly what affected this"), which is an argument that this market currently trades a product whose value the buyer cannot attribute — relevant to Cai's "fog of war" framing from batch 13. No signal held pattern-less for it; `sig-quality-over-quantity-subjective-data` carries the material.
4. **`el-verifiers-law` reuse instead of a new "capability follows measurability" element.** Deliberate, per the reuse-aggressively instruction, and I think correct — they are the same law. But her corollary is genuinely additive and not in the current brief: **the verifiability of a domain is a fact about the domain, not a property of the model**, which reframes "models are good at code" as a category error. **Recommend widening the node's brief at review** and recording the second, independent statement of the law (Cai cited Jason Wei; Branco derives it from product experience without attribution).
5. **`el-pairwise-preference-eval` gains its first qualification.** Batch 12 coined it as the stabilizing move ("don't score, compare"). Branco's finding is that pairwise/preference collection is *also* subject to mean collapse when rater identity isn't modelled — comparison stabilizes the *rating*, but pooling across unmodelled raters still destroys the distribution. Worth adding to the node's brief; it's the difference between a reliable measurement and a representative one.
6. **`el-disagreement-as-data-signal` and `el-expert-commentary-grounding` are merge candidates.** Both are human-QA mechanics for subjective data and could be one node (`el-subjective-data-qa`). I kept them apart because they answer different questions — *how to read consensus* vs *how to score an individual annotation* — and because the code-linking half of the latter is a specific, transferable technique that connects to the video-eval work in batch 12. Merge if you want fewer nodes.
7. **`pat-value-of-judgement` home for `sig-quality-over-quantity-subjective-data`.** The claim is that as execution industrializes, the scarce input becomes high-taste human judgment captured as data — a good fit. If you'd rather keep all five signals on `pat-verification-gap` for coherence, the rehoming is one edge.

3. **The title is a definitional critique, not a counter-take — no ContradictsPattern from this file.** The central brief flagged that "The Dirty Secret" might be a critique of the FDE model, which would make counter-edges welcome. It is not: the "dirty secret" is that the *term* has no coherent referent, and her conclusion is maximally pro-FDE ("everything is trending that way"). She is also the cluster's strongest witness *for* `pat-saaspocalypse` — the outcome-based-pricing argument is the seed pattern's own proof point stated by the vendor named in it. **The genuine counter-take in this set is the Kepler talk** (`ganesh-kepler-fde.md`, which carries one `ContradictsPattern → pat-saaspocalypse`), and the two talks disagree with each other more than either disagrees with the registry: Meurer says FDE is converging with product engineering *and* go-to-market and should be priced on outcomes; Ganesh says treating FDE as go-to-market is precisely the failure mode. That tension is captured in both files rather than smoothed.
4. **FDE thesis proposal (one sentence, NOT coined here):** *As models industrialize execution, the vendor's scarce work moves inside the customer — embedding engineers (and now FDE-support agents) in the customer's own processes to discover, define and guarantee outcomes — which collapses product engineering, services and go-to-market into a single function and drags pricing from seats toward outcomes.* Evidence in this file: the Google/OpenAI build-outs, the composite job posting, "product engineering is also becoming more client-facing", and the seat→usage→outcome progression with FDE as the guarantee mechanism.

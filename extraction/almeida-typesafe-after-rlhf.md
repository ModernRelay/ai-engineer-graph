| `el-assistance-automation-divide` | The assistance / automation divide | concept | training | The talk's explanatory frame for why AI looks simultaneously superhuman and useless. | `el-preference-reward-asymmetry` | Preference reward-model asymmetry | concept | training | The mechanism underneath the divide. Consequences stated as design properties rather than bugs: **overpromising is a feature**, every RLHF model carries a structural gap between human preference and actual results even when the results are good, and **hallucination is intrinsic to optimizing for human preference** rather than a pre-training defect. Illustrated with a ChatGPT response to an audio file of fart noises — "a very eerie vibe atmosphere piece". Stated to be **neither RLHF nor RLVR**, with a **different API shape** from both, and reasoned about from scratch on the analogy that "no one thought about instruction following before we made instruction following happen": a genuinely new post-training branch "looks totally alien, and then in hindsight becomes super obvious". Garry Tan's "golden age of **just-in-time software**" is accepted as accurate and named a double-edged sword: we are automating **the writing** of software while its **expressiveness** stands still, so the building blocks are unchanged.

## Insights (2 new)

| `ins-base-model-is-a-prior-not-a-product` | What died is not the base model, it is the base model **as the ceiling**. When pre-training was the bulk of the compute and RL was a cherry on top, how good your base model was determined how good your model could get, so you built it to reflect as much of human knowledge as possible. Now RL takes an equal or larger share of the budget, so the base model's job is to hold **the atomic skills RL will compose** and to have seen the *shape* of the work — reasoning traces, agentic tasks, test-time-compute schemes — before RL has to discover them. The practical inversion: choose pre-training data by **what the RL phase must compose**, not by what best represents the world. And because the priors are downstream-defined, they are also contingent — today's priors are reasoning and agents because that is how the models get used; **a new interaction paradigm would change what a base model should be** | `HighlightsPattern → pat-accelerated-research` **[registry]** | `ReliesOnElement → el-base-model-as-rl-prior`, `el-pretraining-mix-shift` |
| `ins-distribution-continuity-across-stages` | Every boundary between training stages is a **distribution discontinuity**, and the discontinuities are where the damage happens: MoE experts imbalance when the post-training data looks nothing like pre-training; representations destabilize; capabilities are lost adapting to a domain. The remedy in every recipe examined is the same move in different directions — **blur the boundary**. Nemotron pulls SFT data backwards into pre-training; mid-training pulls the RL distribution and long context backwards ahead of post-training; and `morcos-datology-data-quality-multiplier.md` in this batch pushes the *pre-training* distribution forwards into mid-training to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Two labs, opposite directions, same conclusion: the stage names are an artifact of team boundaries, and treating them as real is what costs you | — (no pattern edge; the cross-file convergence is flagged in Review note 5) | `ReliesOnElement → el-sft-in-pretraining`, `el-mid-training` **[registry]** |

| `how-compose-pretraining-for-rl` | Build the base model as a prior for the RL you intend to run | Work backwards from the RL phase. Ensure the base model has **exposure to the atomic skills it will need to compose** — RL extrapolates from atoms it has seen, but it will not discover them for you — and make the RL environment **difficult enough** that extrapolation is required. Deliberately introduce **novel-shaped data during supervised learning**: shapes the model would not otherwise have seen, such as reasoning traces (which "don't really look like a ton of what humans output") and test-time-compute schemes, so the shape is not new at the moment RL starts. | `how-pull-posttraining-data-earlier` | Move post-training-shaped data backwards, don't patch at the boundary | Put SFT-style question/answer and chat data — and agentic traces, which fit because many models now pre-train at long context anyway — **into the pre-training mix**, so the model learns conversation and task shapes from the start instead of meeting them as a distribution shock later. For MoE models this is also a stability measure: without it, the pre-/post-training distribution gap causes severe expert imbalance, and the available fix (raising the load-balancing coefficient during SFT, as MAI reported) means changing the balance far into training, which you should not want to do.
